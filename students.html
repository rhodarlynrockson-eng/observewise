Student Learning Hub

Build confidence in observation writing through fictional examples, practice activities and reflection.

Use this hub alongside your course materials and your tutor's guidance.

Practise separating facts from assumptions

Read this fictional description:

Child B was being naughty and refused to share because they were angry.

Your task

Which words describe assumptions or judgements? What would you need to see or hear to write an objective account?

Show an example answer

“Naughty” is a judgement. “Refused” suggests an intention, and “angry” interprets a feeling. Describe observable actions and exact words instead.

If you had observed these actions, you could write: “Child B held the truck against their chest and said ‘I'm using it’ when another child reached towards it.”

Build your own practice observation

  1. Imagine a short fictional play scenario. Describe the actions and words without adding judgements.
  2. Include the child's voice if your scenario contains spoken words. Otherwise, describe observable gestures.
  3. Explain what the child may have learnt or demonstrated, using evidence from your description.
  4. Consider relevant areas of learning and development using the framework for your nation and setting.
  5. Suggest a next step that follows the child's interests.

Reflect on your writing

After completing your fictional observation, consider these questions:

  • Which details made my description objective?
  • Did I include any assumptions without evidence?
  • How did I represent the child's voice or actions?
  • What evidence supports my interpretation of learning?
  • What would I improve in my next observation?

Discuss your practice example with your tutor or study group, following your course's expectations.
